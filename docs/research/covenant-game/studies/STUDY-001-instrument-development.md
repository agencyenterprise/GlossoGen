# STUDY-001 — Instrument development

**Status:** complete
**Research program:** covenant-game

## Question

Can a simple, ecologically grounded task create measurable hidden effort and
multi-agent dependence without forcing agents into an effort floor, an effort
ceiling, or structurally redundant verification?

## Scope

This study developed the warehouse-counting instrument. It began with a
counter–verifier design, established that agents respond to economic
incentives, identified a ceiling caused by perfect verification, and moved to a
three-person team-production task with private effort, negotiated payments,
and a temptation gradient.

## Synthesis

The original verifier design could not measure the covenant's value because a
single paid check revealed the answer with certainty. The redesigned task made
three providers genuinely necessary and separated effort, delivery,
correctness, payments, and communication. The final calibration deliberately
cycles through effort-favorable, marginal, and shirking-tempting cases rather
than relying on one average payoff.

## What this study does not establish

Instrument validation does not establish that a covenant improves alignment.
It establishes that later comparisons have meaningful behavioral variation and
interpretable measurements.
