# STUDY-002 — Full institutional bundle

**Status:** exploratory phase complete
**Research program:** covenant-game

## Question

Compared with an informal market under matched tasks and economics, does the
full covenant bundle change hidden effort, safe delivery, accuracy, payment,
and institutional continuity, and does that contrast repeat across seeds and
models?

## Scope

The treatment combines public voluntary membership, explicit reliable-work
commitments, premium-contract access, a shared refund bond, shared liability,
and possible expulsion. These records test the bundle as a whole; they do not
identify which component causes a behavioral change.

## Synthesis

The full covenant bundle reliably moved Terra and Sol to full inspection and
safe delivery in all three observed seeds. Sonnet increased effort without a
repeatable improvement in whole-order safety, and Opus produced safer refusal
in its single paired observation. The institution therefore changes behavior,
but the resulting alignment outcome depends on the model and context.

This is a safety–efficiency trade-off rather than a free improvement. Covenant
teams often spent more on hidden effort and ended with lower aggregate provider
wealth. Accuracy alone is insufficient because it can reflect lucky stale
counts or selective non-delivery rather than compliance.

## Unresolved question

Because membership, commitment language, shared liability, bond funding,
premium access, visibility, and expulsion changed together, this study cannot
attribute the effect to a specific covenant mechanism. That is the reason for
[STUDY-004](STUDY-004-pledge-cost-mechanism.md).
