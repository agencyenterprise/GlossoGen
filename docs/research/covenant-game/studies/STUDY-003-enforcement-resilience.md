# STUDY-003 — Enforcement and resilience

**Status:** exploratory phase complete
**Research program:** covenant-game

## Question

When violations or membership losses occur, can a covenant enforce its
boundaries, compensate clients, preserve productive capacity, deter later
misconduct, and recover financially?

## Experiment records

| Record | Role | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| [EXP-014](../experiments/EXP-014-opportunist-invasion-shock.md) | replacement stress test | The intended stressor did not activate because both replacements complied. |
| [EXP-015](../experiments/EXP-015-scripted-violation-recovery.md) | activation test | The explicit violation instruction was ignored, so recovery was not tested. |
| [EXP-016](../experiments/EXP-016-external-violation-recovery.md) | confirmed-violation test | Supported enforcement and operational recovery; financial repair remained confounded. |
| [EXP-017](../experiments/EXP-017-population-loss-dose-response.md) | dose response | Two-member loss was contained; minimum-size institutions were viable but fragile. |
| [EXP-018](../experiments/EXP-018-graded-enforcement-pilot/experiment.md) | policy pilot | Probation preserved productive capacity after an experienced failure. |
| [EXP-019](../experiments/EXP-019-graded-enforcement-replication/experiment.md) | shared-prefix replication | Repeated immediate compliance and capacity preservation in two trajectories. |

## Synthesis

Refunds can protect clients, expulsions can enforce membership boundaries, and
reserve membership can preserve service after enforcement. These are distinct
outcomes: operational recovery does not imply deterrence, and correct later
orders do not prove that every assignment was inspected. Strict expulsion can
also destroy the minimum workforce mechanically, so fair policy comparisons
must give every arm enough reserve capacity to remain operable.

Graded enforcement preserved capacity in the tested trajectories, but the
evidence does not establish that it is generally superior to strict expulsion.
Natural Sonnet failures later showed that the bond could pay refunds and the
institution could continue with four members, while unsafe delivery still
recurred after enforcement.

## Unresolved questions

- Does sanctioning actually deter future violations over a longer horizon?
- Can voluntary repair restore the bond rather than merely compensate the
  client from accumulated reserves?
- How do newcomers learn and preserve institutional commitments?
