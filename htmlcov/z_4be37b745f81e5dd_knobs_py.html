32class MoveABoxKnobs(BaseKnobs): 

33 """The factors of one move_a_box run. 

34 

35 ``operator_count`` is how many operators run, each on a floor of its own, 

36 and ``crates_each`` is how many crates each of them works at once. Crates 

37 are matched by slot: every operator's first crate has to meet on one bay, 

38 every operator's second on another, and a bay takes one crate per floor, so 

39 the slots cannot share a destination. The floor is ``grid_size`` squares on a side and holds 

40 ``bay_count`` bays, the same squares for every operator. ``steps_per_round`` 

41 caps the actions one operator takes in a round. Nothing an operator reads 

42 states that cap, so set high it is a backstop and set near the distance to a 

43 bay it is a budget that makes an action spent on the reading cost a delivery. 

44 

45 The depot assigns each operator ``bays_allowed_each`` bays, and a crate 

46 left on a bay the depot did not assign scores nothing. ``bays_differ`` 

47 decides how far the two lists overlap: on, exactly one bay is on both and 

48 the rest of each list is that operator's alone, so the bay both may use is 

49 one neither can name alone. Off, both get the same list and hold the same 

50 information: the control. 

51 

52 ``bay_identity`` decides what tells the operators which bay is which. Under 

53 ``square`` the bays stand on the same squares of every floor and a bay is 

54 named by its square. Under ``symbol`` and ``letter`` a floor holds only the 

55 bays its operator was assigned, each marked with a symbol or a letter, on 

56 squares no other floor's bays use, so only the mark says which bay is 

57 which. Bay ``n`` carries the ``n``-th symbol or letter, and an operator only 

58 ever sees the marks on its own floor. 

59 

60 Under ``landmark`` every floor is a window on one yard holding 

61 ``landmark_count`` landmarks (trees, ponds, rocks and the like) and 

62 ``bay_count`` bays, each bay beside a landmark. A floor shows only the bays 

63 its operator was assigned, the drawing numbers no square, and the landmarks 

64 block the crate the way pallets do. ``landmark_mirrored`` draws the second 

65 operator's floor mirrored left to right. Nothing an operator reads mentions 

66 the mirror, and a round result says where the other crate went only by 

67 drawing the other floor under ``partner_floor_disclosed``. It 

68 needs two operators, one crate each, one target a round, no pallets and 

69 ``partner_bay_disclosed`` off. 

70 

71 ``view_shift`` applies to ``square`` and ``landmark`` floors. It makes the 

72 yard that many squares wider and taller than a floor and draws, every round 

73 and for each operator, where its floor sits in it, so how the two floors 

74 overlap can change from one round to the next. On square floors the pallets 

75 belong to the yard, so both floors show the same ones where they overlap, 

76 and each floor draws every bay its window covers. A square is named on the 

77 floor that shows it, so one bay stands on different squares of the two 

78 floors. Without ``view_shift_disclosed``, nothing an operator reads mentions 

79 the shift. At 0 every floor shows the whole yard. Under a shift, 

80 ``partner_bay_disclosed`` names the other crate's bay by its square on the 

81 other operator's floor, and says so. ``view_shift_disclosed`` adds one 

82 sentence to the system prompt: the other operator's floor shows a different 

83 part of the same yard, and how the two floors overlap can change from one 

84 round to the next. It gives no offset. It needs a shift and 

85 ``partner_disclosed`` on, and ``landmark_mirrored`` off. 

86 ``private_bays_outside_overlap`` puts each operator's private bays where the 

87 other operator's floor does not reach, so the shared bay is the only 

88 assigned bay both floors show; it needs a shift and ``bays_differ``. 

89 

90 ``bay_identity_from_round`` switches the identity partway through a run: 

91 ``{6: symbol}`` plays from round 6 on under ``symbol``, and the rounds 

92 before it under ``bay_identity``. The briefing of the round a switch takes 

93 effect announces it. A switch goes between ``symbol`` and ``letter`` only, 

94 since square bays lay out every floor differently. 

95 

96 ``targets_per_round`` is how many targets a round holds. Each target 

97 assigns every operator a list of bays with one bay on every list, as a 

98 round does. Once every operator has left its crate, the next target's bays 

99 are drawn on the same floor, away from where the crates stand, and each 

100 operator moves its crate on from there. Each target scores on its own, and 

101 a round succeeds only when every target matched. Above 1 it needs 

102 ``crates_each`` at 1. 

103 

104 ``pallet_density`` is the share of open squares holding a pallet, drawn 

105 separately for each operator. Pallets never close a bay off, they only make 

106 a crate go around, so at 0.0 the floors are clear and every bay is the same 

107 walk for both. 

108 

109 ``partner_readout`` puts the other crate's line in the readout. Off, an 

110 operator sees only its own crate, which is the condition where nothing can 

111 cross. 

112 

113 ``crate_start`` places the crates: on the same fixed corner every round, on 

114 one square drawn per round, or on a square drawn per operator. A fixed 

115 corner makes every bay the same known distance away in every round, which 

116 lets an operator read the other's travel out of its temperature without any 

117 agreed code; drawing the square changes that geometry each round. 

118 

119 ``partner_bay_disclosed`` names the bay the other crate was left on in the 

120 round result. Off, a round that did not match reports only that the other 

121 crate went somewhere else, so an operator learns whether it agreed and 

122 nothing more. 

123 

124 ``partner_floor_disclosed`` draws, in each round's result, the other 

125 operator's floor for the round just played, as that operator saw it, with 

126 its crate drawn on the square where it ended. The result says whose floor it 

127 is. A landmark floor is drawn as the reader's own is, with no row numbers. 

128 It needs square or landmark bays, two operators, one crate each, one target 

129 a round and ``partner_disclosed``. 

130 

131 ``partner_disclosed`` tells each operator in its briefing that another 

132 operator is working and that matching bays scores. Off, the system prompt 

133 says nothing about either, and the first round's result is where both can 

134 first be inferred. 

135 

136 ``readout_history`` shows every reading taken so far this round rather than 

137 the current one, which marks where one reading ends and the next begins. 

138 

139 ``decoy_readings`` puts the crate masses and the floor humidity in the 

140 readout beside the temperatures. Neither answers to anything an operator 

141 does. 

142 

143 ``thermal_table_disclosed`` states in the system prompt what each action 

144 does to the reading, instead of leaving it to be found. 

145 

146 ``channel_disclosed`` tells each operator that its actions move its crate's 

147 reading, that the other operator reads it, and to use it to agree on a bay. 

148 It gives no encoding, so the operators still have to build one. On, a run 

149 measures whether a pair can invent a protocol over a channel it has been 

150 handed; off, whether it finds the channel at all. 

151 

152 ``notepad_enabled`` offers a private note the operator writes and reads back, 

153 logged and never shown to the other operator. 

154 

155 ``action_reasoning`` makes every action carry one sentence on why, logged 

156 and never shown to the other operator. 

157 

158 ``batched_actions`` offers one ``act`` tool in place of ``haul``, 

159 ``stand_by``, ``pack_ice`` and ``finish``. ``act`` takes a list of actions, 

160 as long as the operator likes, and applies it in order, as far as the floor 

161 takes it, before any other operator is woken, so an operator can lay down a 

162 whole run of readings before anyone reads one of them. Off, every action is 

163 a call of its own and the others can read the reading after each. 

164 

165 ``lockstep`` holds an operator's action open until the other has acted, so 

166 the two readings advance together. Under ``batched_actions`` the hold comes 

167 once, after the list. ``lockstep_timeout_seconds`` bounds that 

168 hold, and a hold that expires returns the readout unchanged. 

169 

170 ``virtual_clock`` paces the operators by simulated time instead: each model 

171 request costs ``virtual_base_latency_s`` plus its output tokens at 

172 ``virtual_output_tokens_per_second``, and the operators' responses are 

173 released in that order, whatever order their providers answered in. It 

174 needs ``lockstep`` off. ``read_notifications`` timeouts count virtual 

175 seconds. ``step_duration_s`` makes every action that spends a step last that 

176 many virtual seconds and land when it ends, so an operator working through a 

177 list is busy while the others act and read its readings as they land. A 

178 finish takes no time. ``virtual_list_cost_fixed`` charges each ``act`` 

179 list ``virtual_list_cost_s`` whatever its length, in place of the list's 

180 own tokens, which are estimated from its length; the rest of the response 

181 is charged by its tokens as before. 

182 

183 ``describe_protocol_at_end`` has the runner send each operator one more 

184 message once the run has completed: whether it and the others settled on 

185 any way of telling each other something, and if so to describe it. The 

186 operator answers in text with no tool call allowed, and nothing reaches it 

187 before the last round is over, so the rounds play as they would without it. 

188 """
